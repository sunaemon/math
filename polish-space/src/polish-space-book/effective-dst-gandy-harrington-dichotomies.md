# Effective DST: Gandy--Harrington Topology and Dichotomies {#sec:effective-dst-dichotomies}
\index{effective descriptive set theory!dichotomy proofs}

<!-- formalization: skip-begin (chapter motivation and proof scope) -->
This chapter proves Silver's dichotomy and the Harrington--Kechris--Louveau embedding theorem, whose classification
consequences were stated in §{sec:borel-reducibility}. The lightface foundations come from §{sec:effective-dst};
boundedness, separation, and uniform codes come from §{sec:effective-dst-ranks}.

The Gandy--Harrington topology turns analytic conditions into neighborhoods for fusion. Silver's proof uses ranks,
generic-pair splitting, and a perfect antichain. HKL then uses an ergodic kernel, invariant separation, and coherent

extensions. §{sec:appendix-forcing-background} develops the general forcing framework for comparison.

## Gandy--Harrington Topology {#sec:edst-gh-topology}

\begin{mathmeta}
  \vars{y,z}{Name}
\end{mathmeta}
\index{Gandy--Harrington topology}

<!--
formalization: skip-begin (引用・背景説明・読書案内:
Silver/HKLの共通topologyを次に構成するという導入。定義と個々のtopological factsは後続の独立した監査対象。)
--> The two dichotomy proofs use effective descriptive set theory as a topology in which analytic conditions support
fusion. We establish the common topological facts here before treating Silver and HKL separately.
\begin{mathmeta}
  \define{GHTopology}
\end{mathmeta}<!--
formalization: skip-end
-->

Use a finer zero-dimensional Polish topology with the same Borel sets as in §{sec:polish-topology-refinement}, and
a closed Baire-space presentation as in §{sec:closed-presentation-baire}. Fix a real parameter $\varepsilon$ coding
this presentation of $X$ and its countable base. The
\termdefine{Gandy--Harrington topology} $\GHTopology$ on $X$ is generated by the lightface
$\lSigma^1_1(\varepsilon)$ subsets of $X$.

\begin{proposition*}[\termdefineas{fixed-parameter basis lemma}{Fixed-parameter bases and dense rectangles}]
On Baire space, the sets with pair-tree presentations at the fixed parameter form a countable basis
$\mathcal B$ for $\GHTopology$. This topology is second countable and refines the ordinary topology. Products of
two basis sets form a basis for the product topology. If $U$ is open and dense in that product, then
$$\mathcal D(U):=\{\Pair{A}{B}\in(\SetDifference{\mathcal B}{\{\emptyset\}})^2:A\times B\IncludedIn U\}$$
is a downward-closed dense set of nonempty rectangle conditions, ordered by coordinatewise inclusion.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
There are countably many finite lambda programs, so there are countably many decidable pair trees
and their projections at a fixed parameter. The projections include the whole space and are closed
under finite intersections. They therefore form a basis for the topology they generate. Every ordinary
cylinder has such a presentation, which proves the refinement assertion. The product-basis theorem
gives the stated rectangle basis.

Downward closure of $\mathcal D(U)$ follows from containment. Given a condition $\Pair{A}{B}$, the rectangle
$A\times B$ is nonempty and open in the product topology. Density of $U$ gives a point in
$U\cap(A\times B)$. Choose a basic rectangle $P\times Q$ containing that point and contained in this
intersection. Its two factors are nonempty, and $\Pair{P}{Q}\leq\Pair{A}{B}$ belongs to $\mathcal D(U)$.
All four factors belong to the original fixed-parameter family; the open dense set itself need not
have an effective code.
\end{proof}

<!-- formalization: require -->
\begin{fact*}[about $\GHTopology$] The following facts hold.

\begin{enumerate}
\item $\GHTopology$ refines the original Polish topology.
\item If $A\IncludedIn X$ is nonempty $\lSigma^1_1(\varepsilon)$, then $A$ is \termuse{strong Choquet} in the relative
$\GHTopology$ topology.
\end{enumerate}

\end{fact*}

\begin{proof}[Proof] For (1), each ordinary basic open set is $\lSigma^0_1(\varepsilon)$ from the code for $X$ and the
chosen base, hence is $\lSigma^1_1(\varepsilon)$. Since these sets are among the generators of $\GHTopology$, every
original open set is $\GHTopology$-open.

For (2), first work on $\BaireSpace$. We construct a winning strategy in the
\termuse{strong Choquet game} of §{sec:topological-infinite-games}. At round $n$, Player I has played
$x_n\in U_n$. For $n>0$, legality gives $x_n\in U_n\IncludedIn V_{n-1}$.
Choose a basic set and a pair tree presenting it:
$$x_n\in B_n\IncludedIn U_n,\qquad B_n=\pi_0(\TreeBody{T_n}).$$
All these tree presentations use the same parameter $\varepsilon$.

Player II remembers the trees already chosen and finite prefixes of the point and of one witness
for each tree. For every $i<n$, membership of $x_n$ in $V_{n-1}$ supplies a witness $y_{i,n}$ through
$T_i$ extending the previously fixed witness prefix. Choose also a witness $y_{n,n}$ through $T_n$ at
$x_n$. Put
$$u_n:=\restrict{x_n}{n+1},\qquad v_{i,n}:=\restrict{y_{i,n}}{n+1}\quad(i\le n).$$
For $n>0$, the point prefix $u_n$ extends $u_{n-1}$, and $v_{i,n}$ extends $v_{i,n-1}$ for each $i<n$.
Thus every old prefix is preserved and gains one coordinate.

Respond with
$$\begin{aligned}
 V_n:=\{z\in\BaireSpace:\ &u_n\preceq z,\quad
  \forall i\le n\ \exists y\in\BaireSpace\\
 &v_{i,n}\preceq y\ \land\ \Pair{z}{y}\in\TreeBody{T_i}\}.
\end{aligned}$$
Finite-prefix restrictions and finite intersections preserve pair-tree presentations at
$\varepsilon$, so $V_n$ is $\lSigma^1_1(\varepsilon)$ and hence open. The chosen witnesses show that
$x_n\in V_n$. The condition for $i=n$ gives $V_n\IncludedIn B_n\IncludedIn U_n$, and preservation of
old prefixes gives $V_n\IncludedIn V_{n-1}$ when $n>0$. This is a legal response. Fix all choices as
functions of the finite history. This defines a strategy; it does not assert a computable procedure
for choosing witnesses from arbitrary points.

In a play following this strategy, the compatible prefixes $u_n$ determine a point $x$, and for
each fixed $i$ the prefixes $v_{i,n}$ for $n\ge i$ determine a witness $y_i$. To justify membership
in the tree body, consider the actual pairs $\Pair{x_n}{y_{i,n}}\in\TreeBody{T_i}$ for $n\ge i$. Their fixed initial
segments grow without bound, so these pairs converge to $\Pair{x}{y_i}$ in the ordinary product topology.
The body $\TreeBody{T_i}$ is closed in that topology, hence $\Pair{x}{y_i}\in\TreeBody{T_i}$. Moreover, $x$ extends
every
$u_n$, and $y_i$ extends $v_{i,n}$ whenever $i\le n$. Thus $x\in V_n\IncludedIn U_n$ for every $n$,
and Player II wins.

Every $\lSigma^1_1(\varepsilon)$ set $A$ is open in $\GHTopology$. The transport lemma in
§{sec:topological-infinite-games} therefore makes $A$ strong Choquet in the relative topology.
For the chosen closed presentation $f:X\to C\IncludedIn\BaireSpace$, the topology on $X$ is generated
by the preimages $f^{-1}[B]$ of the fixed-parameter analytic sets $B$. This is exactly the topology
induced by $f$ from the ambient Gandy--Harrington topology. If $A\IncludedIn X$ is analytic in this
presentation, then $f[A]$ is a fixed-parameter analytic set, and $f$ restricts to a homeomorphism from
$A$ onto $f[A]$ with their relative topologies. Transporting the strategy once more proves (2) on $X$.
\end{proof}

### Coanalytic Ranks and Boundedness {#sec:edst-silver-ranks}

Let $A \IncludedIn X$ be $\lPi^1_1$. A \termdefineas{lightface Pi11 rank}{$\lPi^1_1$ rank} on $A$ is a map
$$\varphi:A\to\omegaOneOrd$$
such that, for each countable ordinal $\alpha$, the section
$$A_{\le\alpha}:=\{x\in A:\varphi(x)\le\alpha\}$$
is Borel. In addition, there are starred relations $\leq_\Sigma$ in $\lSigma^1_1$ and $\leq_\Pi$ in $\lPi^1_1$ such
that, whenever $y\in A$,
$$
x\leq_\Sigma y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\leq_\Pi y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\in A\text{ and }\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y),
$$
and similarly for strict comparison. <!--
formalization: skip-begin (引用・背景説明・読書案内:
lightface rankの全定義とstarred comparisonはEffectiveCoanalyticNormへ接続済み。
未リンク末尾だけはAxA上の比較のanalytic性を追加仮定と誤認しないための注意で、具体的反例の局所証明ではない。)
--> No claim is made that the comparison restricted globally to $A\times A$ is
analytic.<!--
formalization: skip-end
-->

Every $\lPi^1_1$ set admits such a rank. Indeed, reduce $A$ recursively, and hence continuously, to
$\WellFoundedTrees$: choose a recursive map $x\mapsto T_x$ such that
$$x\in A \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T_x\in\WellFoundedTrees.$$
Then set $\varphi(x):=\rho(T_x)$, the well-founded rank of $T_x$. The assertion $\rho(T_x)\le\alpha$ is Borel once
$\alpha$ is fixed, because it is checked by induction through a fixed countable well-order coding $\alpha$. The
starred comparison relations are obtained from strict-extension maps between trees and their well-founded duals, as in
the tree-rank comparison lemma.

<!--
formalization: skip-begin (引用・背景説明・読書案内:

--> Two consequences are used constantly.<!--

-->

\begin{lemma*}[\termuseas{boundedness theorem for well-founded trees}{Rank boundedness for the tree rank}]
If $B\IncludedIn A$ is analytic,
then
$$\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in B\}<\omegaOneOrd.$$
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof] Under the reduction $x\mapsto T_x$, the image of $B$ is analytic inside $\WellFoundedTrees$. Apply
the boundedness theorem for well-founded tree ranks.
\end{proof}

Apply the rank construction to the coanalytic set $\SetDifference{X}{A_1}$ and rank boundedness to its analytic subset
$A_0$. This gives the general-Polish-space version of effective Lusin separation proved in
§{sec:edst-basis-norms-separation}; no second separation theorem is needed here.

For equivalence relations, the rank is applied to the graph. If $E\IncludedIn X\times X$ is $\lPi^1_1$, fix
the tree-rank $\lPi^1_1$
norm $\varphi$ on $E$ and write
$$E_{\le\alpha}:=\{\Pair{x}{y}:x\mathrel E y\text{ and }\varphi(x,y)\le\alpha\}.$$
Each $E_{\le\alpha}$ is Borel, and $E=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omegaOneOrd}E_{\le\alpha}$. Rank boundedness says that
every analytic subset of $E$ is already contained in some Borel approximation $E_{\le\alpha}$.

### The Splitting Argument {#sec:edst-silver-splitting}

\begin{mathmeta}
  \vars{n}{Nat}
  \vars{s}{Seq}
\end{mathmeta}

Let $E$ be a $\lPi^1_1$ equivalence relation on the closed presentation $X$. A set is $E$-homogeneous if all its
points are $E$-equivalent. With the effective codes from §{sec:edst-generic-conditions}, define
$$
H=\SetDifference{X}{\bigcup\{W_e:e\in C,\ W_e\text{ is }E\text{-homogeneous}\}}.
$$
There are only countably many sets in the union. If $H=\emptyset$, then $E$ has at most countably many classes.

\begin{lemma*}[Silver kernel]
The set $H$ is $\lSigma^1_1$. No nonempty $\lSigma^1_1$ subset of $H$ is $E$-homogeneous.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Use $D_e,D'_e$ from the complemented-code construction, so that for valid $e$ they are complements.
Let
$$\begin{aligned}
B_e&:=\{\Pair{u}{v}:u,v\in D_e,\ u\mathrel{\not E}v\},\\
J_e&:=\{x:e\notin C\},\qquad K_e:=\{x:B_e\ne\emptyset\}.
\end{aligned}$$
The relation $\neg E$ has an analytic presentation. Pulling back $D_e$ along both coordinates and
intersecting with that presentation gives a joint tree family for $B_e$. The invalid-code construction
gives $J_e$, and the nonemptiness construction gives $K_e$. All use the fixed parameter. Now
$$H=X\cap\bigcap_e(J_e\cup K_e\cup D'_e).$$
Indeed, an invalid code imposes no restriction. For a valid code, $K_e$ is the whole space precisely
when $D_e$ is not homogeneous. Otherwise the test requires membership in $D'_e$, which excludes $D_e$.
Soundness and completeness of the complemented codes identify this intersection with the definition
of $H$. The uniform union and intersection constructions give the claimed analytic presentation;
restriction to the fixed closed presentation preserves it.

Suppose $A\IncludedIn H$ is nonempty, analytic, and homogeneous. The relational image
$$R_A:=\{y:\exists x\in A\ (x\mathrel{\not E}y)\}$$
is analytic and disjoint from $A$. Effective separation constructs a set $D$ and its complement at
the same parameter, with $A\IncludedIn D\IncludedIn R_A^{\complement}$. Fix $a\in A$.
Every point of $D$ is equivalent to $a$, so symmetry and transitivity make $D$ homogeneous.
Completeness supplies a valid code for this hull. Thus $D$ occurs in the union removed from $H$,
contradicting the nonempty inclusion $A\IncludedIn H\cap D$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{Baire envelope lemma}{Baire envelopes}]
Every subset $A$ of a second-countable space has a superset $B(A)$ with the Baire property such that
$\SetDifference{B(A)}{C}$ is meager whenever $C$ has the Baire property and contains $A$.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Let $U$ be the union of the open sets $V$ for which $A\cap V$ is meager. Second countability reduces this union to a
countable subunion, so $A\cap U$ is meager. Put $B(A)=(\SetDifference{X}{U})\cup(A\cap U)$.
This set contains $A$ and has the Baire property. If $C$ contains $A$ and has the Baire property, choose an open $V$
agreeing with $\SetDifference{B(A)}{C}$ outside a meager set. Then $A\cap V$ is meager, since $A\IncludedIn C$, so
$V\IncludedIn U$.
But $B(A)\cap U\IncludedIn A\IncludedIn C$, hence $V\cap(\SetDifference{B(A)}{C})=\emptyset$. Their agreement outside
the meager
error set shows that $\SetDifference{B(A)}{C}$ is meager.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{closed-projection Baire lemma}{Baire property of closed projections}]
If $Z$ is second countable and $K\IncludedIn Z\times\BaireSpace$ is closed, then $\pi_0(K)$ has the Baire property.
Consequently an analytic or coanalytic subset of a Polish space has the Baire property in every finer
second-countable topology.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
For $s\in\finseq{\omega}$ put $S_s=\pi_0(K\cap(Z\times\cyl{s}))$, so $S_s=\bigcup_kS_{s\concat(k)}$.
Choose Baire envelopes $B_s=B(S_s)$. Each of the sets
$$\SetDifference{B_s}{\Closure{S_s}},\qquad \SetDifference{B_s}{\bigcup_k B_{s\concat(k)}}$$
is meager by the envelope lemma. Remove their union over all $s$. Every remaining $z\in B_\emptyset$ admits a branch
$p$ with $z\in B_{\restrict{p}{n}}\cap\Closure{S_{\restrict{p}{n}}}$ for every $n$. Every product neighborhood of
$\Pair{z}{p}$ then meets $K$: first fix a cylinder
inside its second-coordinate neighborhood, then use $z\in\Closure{S_{\restrict{p}{n}}}$ in the first coordinate.
Closedness gives $\Pair{z}{p}\in K$. Thus $\SetDifference{B_\emptyset}{\pi_0(K)}$ is meager; the reverse difference is
empty,
so the
projection has the Baire property.

For the last assertion, take an ordinary closed witness presentation of the analytic set. The witness set remains
closed when the topology of the first coordinate is refined. Apply the first assertion, and take complements for
coanalytic sets. No claim that the refined topology is Polish is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{coanalytic local truth lemma}{Coanalytic local truth}]
For every coanalytic $S\IncludedIn\BaireSpace^2$, there is a countable family $\mathcal J$ of downward-closed dense
subsets of $\GHConditions{1}\times\GHConditions{1}$ with the following property. If a pair in $S$ is generic for
$\mathcal J$ and belongs to a rectangle $A\times B$, it belongs to a stronger rectangle all of whose
$\mathcal J$-generic pairs belong to $S$.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
The \termuseas{fixed-parameter basis lemma}{basis proposition} shows that the product
Gandy--Harrington topology is second countable and refines the ordinary product topology. By the
closed-projection Baire lemma, $S$ agrees with an open set $U$ outside a meager set. Choose countably
many open dense sets $V_n$ whose intersection avoids that error set. Put
$$\mathcal J:=\{\mathcal D(V_n):n\in\NN\}.$$
The same proposition proves that each of these rectangle tests is downward closed and dense in the
fixed-parameter condition order. Neither choosing the open sets nor listing these dense requirements
is asserted to be computable.

Every $\mathcal J$-generic pair belongs to every $V_n$, so its membership in $S$ is equivalent to membership in $U$.
For a generic pair in $S\cap(A\times B)$, choose a basic rectangle containing the pair and contained in
$U\cap(A\times B)$. Every generic pair in that rectangle belongs to $U$ and therefore to $S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{Silver splitting lemma}{Silver splitting lemma}]
There is a countable family $\mathcal J$ of downward-closed dense subsets of
$\GHConditions{1}\times\GHConditions{1}$ such that every $\mathcal J$-generic pair in $H\times H$ is $E$-inequivalent.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Apply coanalytic local truth to $S=E$, and use its countable family $\mathcal J$. If an equivalent generic pair
belonged to $H\times H$, local truth would give nonempty analytic $A,B\IncludedIn H$ such that every
$\mathcal J$-generic pair in $A\times B$ is $E$-equivalent.
Here individual genericity means meeting the following specified family of dense tests below $A$.
For $D\in\mathcal J$ and each nonempty analytic $B'\IncludedIn B$, put
$$\mathcal L(D,B'):=\{C\in\GHConditions{1}:C\IncludedIn A,\quad
 \exists V\in\GHConditions{1}\ (V\IncludedIn B'\ \land\ C\times V\in D)\}.$$
This family is dense below $A$: refine any rectangle $C_0\times B'$ into $D$ and take its left coordinate.
It is downward closed because $D$ is. Let $\mathcal F$ consist of all these families. It is countable,
since both the analytic condition order and $\mathcal J$ are countable.
Any two individually generic points $x,x'\in A$ have a common point $y\in B$ such that both $\Pair{x}{y}$ and
$\Pair{x'}{y}$
are product generic. To see this explicitly, take a downward-closed dense family $D\in\mathcal J$ of rectangles.
For every analytic $B'\IncludedIn B$, the point $x$ meets $\mathcal L(D,B')$.
This supplies a rectangle from $D$ whose left coordinate contains $x$ and whose right coordinate lies in $B'$.
Thus the admissible right conditions for $x$ are dense below $B$; the same holds for $x'$.
Alternately meet these two countable lists of requirements, including the witness requirements of
analytic-condition fusion. The common right limit is the required $y$. Transitivity now gives $x\mathrel E x'$.

The Silver-kernel lemma says that
$$R=\SetDifference{(A\times A)}{E}$$
is nonempty. It is lightface analytic because $E$ is coanalytic.
Apply the \termuse{generic coordinates of an analytic pair condition} proposition in
§{sec:edst-generic-conditions} to $R$ and the family $\mathcal F$ of local projection tests just defined.
Both projections of $R$ lie in $A$, and every member of $\mathcal F$ is dense below $A$, so the proposition
gives an actual pair $\Pair{u}{v}\in R$ whose coordinates meet every test in $\mathcal F$.
The common-right-point argument therefore gives $u\mathrel E v$.
But actual membership in $R$ gives $u\mathrel{\not E}v$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The conclusion concerns generic pairs of actual members of the conditions. It makes no assertion about all pairs
in their ordinary closures. The distinction between analytic pair conditions and product conditions is essential:
the contradictory pair in $R$ need only have individually generic coordinates.

### Fusion and the Perfect Antichain {#sec:edst-silver-fusion}

\begin{mathmeta}
  \vars{a,b}{Name}
  \vars{i,n}{Nat}
  \vars{s,t}{Seq(Nat)}
\end{mathmeta}

\begin{theorem*}[Silver fusion]
If a coanalytic equivalence relation on a Polish space has uncountably many classes, it admits a continuous injection
of $\CantorSpace$ whose distinct images are inequivalent.
\end{theorem*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
First work on Baire space. Code pairs by coordinatewise numerical pairing, choose a closed witness
presentation of the nonrelation of $E$, and put its finite node tests in one parameter as in
§{sec:edst-generic-conditions}. The kernel construction then gives $H$ a pair-tree presentation at
that same parameter, and uncountably many classes imply $H\ne\emptyset$. Thus the kernel itself is
a condition in the fixed-parameter order. Enumerate the node witness tests and the product tests
from the splitting lemma as $\Sequence{D_n}{n\in\omega}$ and $\Sequence{J_n}{n\in\omega}$. Replace each family by the
intersection of its first
$n+1$ members, and include diameter at most $2^{-n}$ in $D_n$.

Construct nonempty analytic sets $A_s$ for $s\in\finseq{2}$, beginning with $A_\emptyset\IncludedIn H$, so that
$$
A_{s\concat(i)}\IncludedIn A_s,\qquad A_s\in D_{\Length{s}},\qquad
A_s\times A_t\in J_n\quad(s\ne t\in 2^n).
$$
At a new level, copy each old condition twice. Refine each of the finitely many node conditions into $D_n$ and
then each ordered rectangle of distinct nodes into $J_n$. Ordinary product forcing allows its two coordinates to
be refined independently. Every later refinement preserves previously achieved requirements because the dense
families are downward closed. All node conditions remain nonempty subsets of $H$.

Analytic-condition fusion gives a unique point
$$\pi(a)\in\bigcap_n A_{\restrict{a}{n}}$$
for each $a\in\CantorSpace$. If two branches agree through length $n$, their points belong to the same set of
diameter at most $2^{-n}$; hence $\pi$ is continuous. If $a\ne b$, their node labels are distinct at every sufficiently
large level. The sequence of their rectangles therefore meets every $J_n$, so $\Pair{\pi(a)}{\pi(b)}$ is product
generic.
The splitting lemma gives $\pi(a)\mathrel{\not E}\pi(b)$, which also proves injectivity.

For a general Polish $X$ with uncountably many classes, choose the continuous surjection
$q:\BaireSpace\to X$ constructed in §{sec:universal-continuous-surjection}. Pull $E$ back along $q$. The resulting
relation is coanalytic and still has uncountably many classes, because the induced map of quotients is surjective.
Apply the Baire-space construction. The composite $q\circ\pi$ remains an antichain and is injective: equality of two
images would make the corresponding points equivalent. Its image is compact and homeomorphic to Cantor space,
hence is a nonempty perfect antichain. Conversely, any Cantor antichain injects Cantor space into the quotient, so the
countable-class and perfect-antichain alternatives are mutually exclusive.
\end{proof}

### The Ergodic Kernel and Refinement Lemmas {#sec:edst-hkl-kernel}

\begin{mathmeta}
  \define{ERSaturation,EffectiveClosureER,RestrictedGHProduct}
  \vars{n}{Nat}
  \vars{b}{Nat}
\end{mathmeta}
For an equivalence relation $F$, write
$$\ERSaturation{A}{F}=\{y:\exists x\in A\ (x\mathrel F y)\}$$
for the saturation of $A$. If $F$ and $A$ are lightface analytic, so is this saturation.

\begin{proposition*}[\termdefineas{relational image index transformation}{Relational images on indices}]
Fix a relative pair-tree presentation of a relation $R$. There is a total numerical lambda-computable
function $h$ such that, for every analytic index $e$,
$$A_{h(e)}=\{y:\exists x\in A_e\ R(x,y)\}.$$
The parameter is unchanged, and no totality test on the program indexed by $e$ is used.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Use the uniform indexed tree for $A_e$. Pull back its first word by the first numerical projection,
intersect with the fixed tree presenting $R$, and project to the second point coordinate, as in
§{sec:edst-coordinate-projections}. In every finite computation retain $e$ alongside the node code.
List mapping, witness pairing, finite tabulation, and the bounded prefix tests therefore give a joint
decider for the resulting family. Its projection is exactly the displayed relational image by the three
body identities. Specializing this decider by $e$ supplies $h$ through the lambda syntax transformation
of §{sec:edst-pair-tree-indices}. Thus the uniformity follows from a concrete program, not from separate
choices of presentations for the images.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{effective invariant separation lemma}{Effective invariant separation}]
Let $F$ be a lightface analytic equivalence relation, and let $A,B$ be disjoint lightface analytic sets
at the same parameter. If $B$ is $F$-invariant, there is an $F$-invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set $C$ with
$A\IncludedIn C\IncludedIn B^{\complement}$. In particular, this applies when both input sets are invariant.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
In this proof write $P_e$ for the uniform analytic presentations. Choose $a,b$ with $A=P_a$, $B=P_b$.
Let $s,t$ be the separation-index transformations and let $h$ be the relational-image transformation for $F$.
Define numerical indices by the recursion
$$\begin{aligned}
a_0&:=a,\\
c_n&:=s(\NatPair{a_n}{b}),\qquad d_n:=t(\NatPair{a_n}{b}),\\
a_{n+1}&:=h(c_n),\qquad A_n:=P_{a_n},\qquad C_n:=P_{c_n}.
\end{aligned}$$
Composition and the closed lambda recursor make $n\mapsto a_n,c_n,d_n$ total lambda-computable functions.
The explicit terms are given in §{sec:lambda-lusin-image-programs}. Hence the trees for $C_n$ and $P_{d_n}$
are jointly decidable in $n$ and their finite nodes.

Inductively, $A_n$ is disjoint from $B$, separation gives
$$A_n\IncludedIn C_n\IncludedIn B^{\complement},\qquad P_{d_n}=C_n^{\complement},$$
and $A_{n+1}=\ERSaturation{C_n}{F}$. Indeed, a point of $B$ related to a point of $C_n$ would, by
invariance of $B$, put that point of $C_n$ in $B$, a contradiction. Reflexivity gives
$C_n\IncludedIn A_{n+1}$, so
$$C:=\bigcup_n C_n=\bigcup_n A_n,\qquad C^{\complement}=\bigcap_n P_{d_n}.$$
The uniform countable-operation constructions of §{sec:edst-uniform-countable-operations} give analytic
presentations of both sides at the same parameter. Finally, if $x\in C_n$ and $x\mathrel F y$, then
$y\in A_{n+1}\IncludedIn C_{n+1}$. Symmetry gives the reverse implication, so $C$ is invariant.
It contains $A_0=A$, and every $C_n$ avoids $B$. This proves the conclusion; invariance of $A$ was not needed.
\end{proof}

Let $\mathcal I$ be the family of $E$-invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ sets at the fixed parameter.
Define the auxiliary equivalence relation by
$$x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall D\in\mathcal I\ (x\in D\ \Longleftrightarrow\ y\in D).$$

It contains $E$ and is Borel, being the intersection of countably many Borel tests.
To construct its analytic presentation at the fixed parameter, put
$$\begin{aligned}
B_e&:=\{\Pair{u}{v}:u\in D_e,\ v\in D'_e,\ u\mathrel E v\},\\
J_e&:=\{p:e\notin C\},\qquad K_e:=\{p:B_e\ne\emptyset\},\\
L_e&:=(D'_e\times X\ \cup\ X\times D_e)
       \cap(X\times D'_e\ \cup\ D_e\times X).
\end{aligned}$$
For valid $e$, nonemptiness of $B_e$ is exactly failure of invariance of $D_e$. One implication follows
from a pair leaving $D_e$; for the other, symmetry of $E$ turns either direction of failed invariance
into such a pair. Also, membership of $\Pair{x}{y}$ in $L_e$ says exactly that both points agree on membership
in $D_e$. Consequently the graph is
$$\EffectiveClosureER{E}=\bigcap_e(J_e\cup K_e\cup L_e),$$
where pairs are coded as points of Baire space. Each family on the right has a joint tree decider:
use coordinate inverse images, intersections, and the nonemptiness operation for $B_e,K_e$, and
binary unions and intersections for $L_e$. Invalidity is handled by $J_e$. Uniform countable
intersection therefore gives an analytic presentation of the actual invariant-test relation, without
a procedure selecting the valid invariant codes.

The relation $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$ is exactly the closure of $E$ in the product of the two
Gandy--Harrington topologies. A separating invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set gives disjoint basic neighborhoods with no
$E$-pair between them. Conversely, if analytic neighborhoods $A,B$ have no such pair, their $E$-saturations are
disjoint analytic invariant sets; invariant separation gives a $\lDelta^1_1$ invariant set distinguishing every
point of $A$ from every point of $B$.

If $E=\EffectiveClosureER{E}$, enumerate the invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ sets as $\Sequence{B_n}{n\in\omega}$. The Borel map
$x\mapsto\Sequence{\mathbf 1_{B_n}(x)}{n\in\omega}$ reduces $E$ to equality on Cantor space, so $E$ is smooth. The
enumeration need not
be effective: the conclusion here is ordinary Borel smoothness. For a nonsmooth $E$, therefore, the
\termdefine{ergodic kernel}
$$H=\{x:\exists y\ (x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y\ \land\ x\mathrel{\not E}y)\}$$
is nonempty. It is lightface analytic and invariant under $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$, hence also under $E$.

\begin{lemma*}[Hereditary failure of separation]
For every nonempty lightface analytic $A\IncludedIn H$, the restrictions of $E$ and $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$ to
$A$ are different.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Suppose they agree on $A$. Put $Y=\ERSaturation{A}{E}$ and $Z=\ERSaturation{A}{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}$.
They also agree on $Y$: given two points of $Y$, move each back into $A$ along $E$ and use transitivity.
Under this assumption the set
$$Y'=\{z:\exists a\in A\ (a\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}z\ \land\ a\mathrel{\not E}z)\}$$
is exactly $\SetDifference{Z}{Y}$: if $z\in Y$, any two such representatives from $A$ would be
$\EffectiveClosureER{E}$-equivalent and therefore $E$-equivalent. Thus $Y,Y'$ are disjoint analytic invariant sets.
If $Y'$ were nonempty, invariant separation would give an invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set containing $Y$ and disjoint
from $Y'$. But every point of $Y'$ is $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$-equivalent to a point of $A\IncludedIn Y$, a
contradiction. Hence $Z=Y$. Agreement on $Y$ now says that every $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$-class meeting $A$ is a
single $E$-class, contradicting $A\IncludedIn H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[Disjoint compatible refinements]
If nonempty analytic $A,B\IncludedIn H$ satisfy $\ERSaturation{A}{E}=\ERSaturation{B}{E}$, there are disjoint
nonempty analytic $A'\IncludedIn A$, $B'\IncludedIn B$ with equal $E$-saturations.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
First there are distinct $a\in A,b\in B$ with $a\mathrel E b$. Otherwise equality of saturations forces $A=B$ and
makes $E$ equality on $A$. Hereditary failure gives $x,y\in A$ with
$x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y$ and $x\mathrel{\not E}y$. In particular they are distinct. Choose disjoint
clopen cylinders $U,V$ containing them. The saturations of $A\cap U$ and $A\cap V$ are disjoint:
a common point would give two $E$-equivalent points of $A$ in disjoint cylinders, contradicting equality on
$A$. Invariant separation supplies an invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set containing the first saturation and
avoiding the second. It separates $x,y$, contrary to the definition of $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$.

Choose disjoint clopen neighborhoods $U,V$ of such $a,b$ and put
$$A_0=A\cap U,\qquad B_0=B\cap V,\qquad
A'=A_0\cap\ERSaturation{B_0}{E},\qquad
B'=B_0\cap\ERSaturation{A_0}{E}.$$
Both sets are nonempty and analytic. Their saturations both equal
$\ERSaturation{A_0}{E}\cap\ERSaturation{B_0}{E}$, and they are disjoint.
\end{proof}

Let $\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$ consist of rectangles $A\times B$ with $A,B\in\GHConditions{1}$ and
$\ERSaturation{A}{E}=\ERSaturation{B}{E}$, again ordered by inclusion. If $A'$ is a nonempty analytic subset of
$A$, replace $B$ by $B\cap\ERSaturation{A'}{E}$. This is nonempty and restores equality of saturations.
Consequently coordinate projections of generic filters for this partial order are
$\GHConditions{1}$-generic, and its generic pair belongs to every rectangle in the filter.

\begin{proposition*}[\termdefineas{restricted-product generic filters}{Actual pairs in the restricted product}]
Let $\mathcal J$ be a countable family of downward-closed dense subsets of $\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$.
Below each starting rectangle $P_0$, there is a filter meeting every member of $\mathcal J$ and the
coordinate witness requirements. Exactly one pair belongs to every rectangle of this filter, and this
pair belongs to a rectangle in each member of $\mathcal J$.
\end{proposition*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
For each witness requirement on one coordinate, take the rectangles whose corresponding coordinate
belongs to that requirement. These families are downward-closed. They are dense because a prescribed
coordinate refinement can be retained exactly while the other coordinate is restricted by its saturation.
Adjoin these countably many families to $\mathcal J$, and enumerate the result as $\Sequence{D_n}{n\in\omega}$.
Starting from $P_0$, choose $P_{n+1}\le P_n$ in $D_n$. The upward closure of this decreasing sequence is a
filter: any two of its members have a common stronger member farther along the sequence.

Apply analytic-condition fusion separately to the two coordinate sequences. This gives unique points
$x,y$ belonging to every chosen coordinate condition. Hence $\Pair{x}{y}$ belongs to every rectangle of the
filter, including those outside the sequence. Conversely, any pair belonging to every filter member
belongs to every chosen rectangle, so coordinate uniqueness identifies it with $\Pair{x}{y}$.
For each $D_n$, the rectangle $P_{n+1}$ witnesses the required encounter.
The sequence is chosen using density; its coordinate presentations retain the fixed parameter through
the intersection and saturation programs already constructed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[Coordinate decisions and point filters]
For each $S\in\GHConditions{1}$, define two families of restricted rectangles by
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal C_0(S)&:=\{A\times B\in\RestrictedGHProduct{E}:
 A\IncludedIn S\ \lor\ A\cap S=\emptyset\},\\
\mathcal C_1(S)&:=\{A\times B\in\RestrictedGHProduct{E}:
 B\IncludedIn S\ \lor\ B\cap S=\emptyset\}.
\end{aligned}$$
These families are downward closed and dense, and the family of all of them is countable.
If an actual pair $\Pair{x}{y}$ belongs to a rectangle in each of these families, both coordinates decide every
lightface analytic set, $x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y$, and all restricted rectangles containing $\Pair{x}{y}$
form a filter under refinement.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
For density of $\mathcal C_0(S)$, start with $A\times B$. If $A\cap S$ is empty, this rectangle already passes.
Otherwise retain $A\cap S$ as the left coordinate and replace the right coordinate by
$B\cap\ERSaturation{A\cap S}{E}$. Equality of the original saturations makes this nonempty and restores
equality of the two new saturations. The right-coordinate argument is symmetric. Every further shrinking
preserves either decision, and there are only countably many analytic conditions $S$.

Meeting these rectangle tests makes each coordinate meet the analytic decision tests of
§{sec:edst-generic-conditions}. Let $D$ be an invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set. A rectangle meeting the left decision
test for $D$ has its left coordinate contained in $D$ or disjoint from $D$. Equal $E$-saturations and
invariance of $D$ force the same alternative for its right coordinate. Thus $x\in D$ if and only if $y\in D$;
the empty-set case is automatic. This proves $x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y$.

More generally, any two points $x,y$ that decide every lightface analytic set and satisfy
$x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y$ agree on all invariant lightface analytic sets.
If $x\in A$ and $y\notin A$ for such an invariant $A$, the decision test at $y$ gives
an analytic neighborhood $B$ of $y$ disjoint from $A$. Its saturation is still disjoint from $A$.
Invariant separation then gives an invariant $\lDelta^1_1$ set distinguishing $x$ from $y$, a contradiction.

Now let $A_0\times B_0$ and $A_1\times B_1$ be restricted rectangles containing $\Pair{x}{y}$, and put
$$C:=A_0\cap A_1,\qquad D:=B_0\cap B_1.$$
Both are nonempty analytic conditions. Their saturations are invariant analytic sets, so the preceding
invariant-agreement argument gives $x\in\ERSaturation{D}{E}$ and $y\in\ERSaturation{C}{E}$. Consequently
$$(C\cap\ERSaturation{D}{E})\times(D\cap\ERSaturation{C}{E})$$
is a restricted rectangle containing $\Pair{x}{y}$ and refining both original rectangles. Its two saturations
are $\ERSaturation{C}{E}\cap\ERSaturation{D}{E}$. This proves directedness. Upward closure follows from
containment of the pair, and $X\times X$ makes the family nonempty. This constructs the required filter
directly from pointwise test encounters.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{HKL generic-pair lemma}{HKL generic-pair lemma}]
There is a countable family $\mathcal J$ of downward-closed dense subsets of $\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$ such that
every pair in $H\times H$ belonging to some rectangle in each member of $\mathcal J$ is $E$-inequivalent.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
For this Borel relation we can prove the needed local truth property directly. Fix a well-founded Borel code
for $E$ whose leaves are basic cylinder rectangles. For each set $S$ of conditions closed under strengthening,
the decision family
$$D(S)=S\cup\{P:\forall Q\le P\ (Q\notin S)\}$$
is downward-closed and dense. At a leaf, $P$ forces membership when it is contained in that cylinder rectangle.
At a complement node, $P$ forces membership when no stronger condition forces the child. At a union node,
$P$ forces membership when conditions forcing one of the children are dense below $P$. Include the decision
family for the child at each complement node and for the union of the children's forcing sets at each union node.
There are only countably many such tests.

Include all the families $\mathcal C_i(S)$ from the preceding lemma in the Borel truth tests.
That lemma constructs the filter of all restricted rectangles containing the actual pair and proves that
its coordinates decide analytic sets and are $\EffectiveClosureER{E}$-related. Applying the same saturation
restriction to two basic cylinder neighborhoods gives a condition in this filter contained in their rectangle,
which proves atomic truth. Induction on the Borel code now proves that an actual generic pair belongs to the decoded
set exactly when some condition of its filter forces membership. For a complement, its decision test chooses
the negative alternative exactly when child membership fails. For a union, directedness and its decision test
turn density below a filter condition into a positive child condition in the filter. Consequently an equivalent
pair meeting this countable family lies in a rectangle all of whose pairs meeting the same family are equivalent.

Suppose a rectangle $U\times V$ below $H\times H$ has only equivalent pairs meeting a fixed countable family of
downward-closed dense tests. We claim that individually generic $x,y\in U$ with
$x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y$ must then be $E$-equivalent, with the following individual tests.
First enlarge the rectangular family by the lifts of the right-coordinate witness requirements from
analytic-condition fusion. This countable family is still downward closed and dense. For each restricted
rectangle $P=C\times D_0$ and each member $J$ of this enlarged family, put
$$\mathcal P(P,J):=\{C'\in\GHConditions{1}:\exists D'\in\GHConditions{1}
 \ (C'\times D'\le P\ \land\ C'\times D'\in J)\}.$$
This is dense below $C$: retain a prescribed left refinement, restrict the right coordinate by its saturation,
and refine the resulting rectangle into $J$. Extend it to the whole coordinate order by allowing conditions
disjoint from $C$ or contained in some member of $\mathcal P(P,J)$. Intersecting with $C$ proves that this
extension is dense; a point of $C$ meeting it must take the second alternative. These extensions, together
with all analytic decision tests, form a countable family $\mathcal F$ because the restricted condition
order and the enlarged rectangular family are countable. Require $x,y$ to meet every member of $\mathcal F$.
Enumerate the enlarged rectangular tests as
$\Sequence{J_n}{n\in\omega}$. Construct two sequences of nested rectangles $U_n\times V_n$ and $U'_n\times V_n$, whose
left conditions contain $x,y$ respectively and whose saturations agree. Start both sequences with $U\times V$.

For the next dense family $J_n$, genericity of $x$ gives a refinement $A\times B\in J_n$ below
$U_n\times V_n$ with $x\in A$. Indeed, left projections of such refinements are dense below $U_n$: any
nonempty analytic subset of $U_n$ can be paired with its saturation restriction in $V_n$ and then refined into
$J_n$. Put $A'=U'_n\cap\ERSaturation{A}{E}$. The invariant-agreement argument ensures $y\in A'$.
For the saturation equality, use $\ERSaturation{U'_n}{E}=\ERSaturation{U_n}{E}$ and $A\IncludedIn U_n$ to obtain
$$\ERSaturation{A'}{E}=\ERSaturation{U'_n}{E}\cap\ERSaturation{A}{E}
=\ERSaturation{A}{E}=\ERSaturation{B}{E}.$$
The last equality holds because $A\times B$ is a restricted rectangle.
Similarly choose $C\times D\in J_n$ below $A'\times B$ with
$y\in C$. Finish the stage with
$$U_{n+1}=A\cap\ERSaturation{D}{E},\qquad U'_{n+1}=C,\qquad V_{n+1}=D.$$
The invariant-agreement argument again retains $x$, since $y\in\ERSaturation{D}{E}$.
Both new rectangles are in $J_n$ and have equal coordinate saturations.
Include the analytic-condition witness requirements in this construction.
Their common right limit $z$ makes both $\Pair{x}{z}$ and $\Pair{y}{z}$ generic below $U\times V$. Thus $x\mathrel E z$
and
$y\mathrel E z$, proving the claim.

Hereditary failure now gives a nonempty analytic pair condition
$$R=\{\Pair{x}{y}\in U^2:x\mathrel{\EffectiveClosureER{E}}y\ \land\ x\mathrel{\not E}y\}.$$
Restrict the family $\mathcal F$ to conditions below $U$. Its members remain dense there.
The \termuse{generic coordinates of an analytic pair condition} proposition in §{sec:edst-generic-conditions}
applied to $R$ gives an actual pair in $R$ whose coordinates meet every one of these individual tests.
This pair contradicts the common-right-point claim.
Thus no kernel rectangle has only equivalent pairs meeting any prescribed countable
family of downward-closed dense tests. Apply this to the Borel truth tests above. If a pair in $H\times H$
meeting them were equivalent, local truth and a common refinement with $H\times H$ would give just such a
rectangle. This contradiction proves the lemma with that family as $\mathcal J$.
\end{proof}

### Coherent Fusion and the Embedding {#sec:edst-hkl-fusion}

\begin{mathmeta}
  \vars{n}{Nat}
\end{mathmeta}

For each $n$, make $2^n$ a finite tree by joining
$$0^k\concat(0)\concat w\quad\text{to}\quad0^k\concat(1)\concat w
\qquad(k<n,\ \Length{w}=n-k-1).$$
Call these the \termdefineas{crucial pair}{crucial pairs}, oriented from $0$ to $1$ at the displayed coordinate.
At level $n+1$ there are two copies of the level-$n$ tree, obtained by appending the last bit, joined by the new edge
$\Pair{0^n\concat(0)}{0^n\concat(1)}$. Induction proves that the graph is connected and has no cycles.

A coherent system on this tree consists of nonempty analytic node sets $A_s\IncludedIn H$ and nonempty analytic
edge relations $R_{s,t}\IncludedIn E\cap(A_s\times A_t)$ whose coordinate projections are exactly $A_s,A_t$.
Connectedness implies that all node sets have the same $E$-saturation.

\begin{lemma*}[\termdefineas{finite-tree refinement lemma}{Finite-tree refinement}]
A coherent system can be refined after any one node is replaced by a prescribed nonempty analytic subset,
retaining exactly that subset at the chosen node. It can also be refined after any one edge is replaced by a
prescribed nonempty analytic subrelation, retaining exactly that subrelation and its two coordinate projections
at the chosen edge and endpoints. All other node sets and edge relations are only shrunk.
\end{lemma*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
Root the tree at the changed node, and put the prescribed subset there. If $u$ has been processed and $v$
is its next neighbor away from the root, define
$$A'_v:=\pi_1(R_{u,v}\cap(A'_u\times X)).$$
Every point of $A'_u$ has a partner in the old relation, so this set is nonempty. Restricting the edge to
$A'_u\times A'_v$ preserves exactly these two projections. Continue along the unique paths from the root,
and finally restrict every old edge to its new endpoints. The unique-path property ensures consistency.
In particular the chosen root set remains unchanged.

Now prescribe a nonempty subrelation $P\IncludedIn R_{u,v}$, and put
$$C:=\pi_0(P),\qquad D:=\pi_1(P).$$
First propagate $C$ from $u$ as above. Every pair of $P$ survives the restriction of the edge, since its first
coordinate lies in $C$ and its second lies in the propagated set at $v$. Next propagate $D$ from $v$ in this
refined system. The new set at $u$ is contained in $C$. Conversely, each point of $C$ has a partner in $D$
through $P$; the reverse edge retains that pair, so the point survives propagation back to $u$.
Thus the endpoint sets are exactly $C,D$, and the refined edge still contains $P$. Replace it by $P$ and
its reverse by the swapped relation. Both projections remain full, and all other edges stay coherent.
All changes are restrictions, so previously satisfied downward-closed requirements are preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary*}[\termdefineas{fixed-parameter tree refinement}{Fixed-parameter tree refinement}]
Suppose the node sets, edge relations, and prescribed shrinking set or subrelation have pair-tree
presentations at the fixed parameter. The preceding refinements retain such presentations at every
node and edge. For each fixed finite path, propagation along that path is a total lambda-computable
transformation of analytic indices.
\end{corollary*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
For one step, restrict an edge by taking the inverse image of the new node set under the first point
projection and intersecting with the edge relation. Project to the second coordinate. These are the
lambda tree operations of §{sec:edst-coordinate-projections}. Iterating them along a finite path gives
its propagated set; equivalently, compose the relational-image index transformations from
§{sec:edst-hkl-kernel}. The empty path uses the identity transformation. Every input index is allowed
because the delayed tree presentation is defined on all indices.

To restrict both endpoints, take both coordinate inverse images and intersect them with the edge.
To reverse an edge, apply numerical coordinate swap to every entry of its coded point. List mapping
and numerical pairing compute this inverse image. Thus the two propagation passes and the final edge
replacement preserve the original parameter. The explicit swap and path-composition terms are given
in §{sec:lambda-lusin-image-programs}. Nonemptiness and containment are hypotheses on the chosen
shrinking sets; the finite programs do not test these properties.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem*}[\termdefineas{Harrington--Kechris--Louveau embedding}{HKL embedding; Glimm--Effros form}]
If $E$ is a nonsmooth Borel equivalence relation on a Polish space $X$, there is a continuous injection
$\pi:\CantorSpace\to X$ such that
$$a\mathrel{\EventualEqualityER}b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\pi(a)\mathrel E\pi(b).$$
\end{theorem*}

\begin{proof}[Proof]
First work on Baire space and its nonempty kernel $H$. Enumerate decreasing sequences $(D_n),(K_n),(J_n)$
of downward-closed dense families for $\GHConditions{1}$, $\GHConditions{2}$, and $\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$,
respectively. Use the countable witness families from analytic-condition fusion for the first two orders and
the family from the HKL generic-pair lemma for the third, taking successive finite intersections. Include
diameter at most $2^{-n}$ in $D_n$.

Construct coherent systems at all levels with the following additional requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $A_{s\concat(i)}\IncludedIn A_s$ and $A_s\in D_n$ for $s\in2^n$;
\item $R_{s\concat(i),t\concat(i)}\IncludedIn R_{s,t}$ for every old crucial pair, and
      $R_{s,t}\in K_n$ for every crucial pair at level $n$;
\item whenever $s,t\in2^n$ have different last bits,
      $A_s\times A_t\in J_n$ and $A_s\cap A_t=\emptyset$.
\end{enumerate}

Start with any $A_\emptyset\in D_0$ contained in $H$. Given level $n$, first duplicate its sets and relations into
the two last-bit copies. On the new joining edge put the full relation
$$R_{0^n\concat(0),0^n\concat(1)}
   =E\cap(A_{0^n\concat(0)}\times A_{0^n\concat(1)}).$$
Initially its endpoint sets are equal, so reflexivity gives full projections. The duplicated system is coherent.
The refinements are performed in the following order.

First refine every node into $D_{n+1}$, one at a time, using finite-tree refinement. During this step keep the new
joining edge equal to the full $E$-relation between its endpoint sets. Propagation across a full $E$-edge preserves
that property: restricting one endpoint and taking the other projection replaces the latter by its intersection
with the former's saturation.

Next handle every ordered pair of nodes with different last bits. Removing the joining edge separates these two
nodes. Their rectangle is a condition in $\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$, so choose a refinement into $J_{n+1}$; apply
disjoint compatible refinements to its two coordinates as well. Propagate these two changes separately through
the two components. Within either component, every edge lies in $E$ and has full projections, so all the resulting
node sets have the saturation of the chosen starting set. The two starting sets had equal saturations. Hence the
new endpoint sets at the joining edge still have equal saturations, and the full $E$-relation between them has full
projections. Restoring that full relation is a restriction of the old joining relation. This proves that the two
propagations are compatible. Repeat for the finitely many pairs. Downward closure preserves all earlier dense
requirements and all earlier disjointness requirements.

Finally refine each edge relation into $K_{n+1}$ and propagate its projections into the two components as in the
finite-tree refinement lemma. At this last step the joining edge need no longer remain a full $E$-relation.
All node and rectangle requirements already obtained persist under restriction. Every old copied edge has only
been restricted, so its parent-containment requirement holds too. This completes the induction and proves that
all three requirements can be achieved simultaneously.

For each branch $a$, analytic-condition fusion gives a unique actual member
$$\pi(a)\in\bigcap_n A_{\restrict{a}{n}}.$$
Shrinking diameters give continuity. Distinct branches differ at some coordinate, and the corresponding level's
node sets are disjoint by requirement (3), so $\pi$ is injective.

Suppose first that $a$ and $b$ differ infinitely often. At the unboundedly many levels where their last bits
differ, their rectangle lies in $J_n$. Rectangles at all intervening levels remain conditions of
$\RestrictedGHProduct{E}$ because the level system is coherent. Thus the descending rectangle sequence is generic,
and the HKL generic-pair lemma gives $\pi(a)\mathrel{\not E}\pi(b)$.

For the other direction, first take
$$a=0^k\concat(0)\concat c,\qquad b=0^k\concat(1)\concat c.$$
Their prefixes form a crucial pair at every level after $k$. By requirement (2), the corresponding edge relations
form a decreasing sequence meeting every $K_n$. Analytic-condition fusion in $X^2$ gives an actual pair in all of
these relations. Its coordinates must be $\pi(a),\pi(b)$ by node uniqueness. Since every edge relation is contained
in $E$, this proves $\pi(a)\mathrel E\pi(b)$.

Finally, if $a\mathrel{\EventualEqualityER}b$, choose $m$ after their last disagreement and write
$a=s\concat c$, $b=t\concat c$ with $s,t\in2^m$. The crucial-pair tree on $2^m$ has a finite path from $s$ to $t$.
Appending the same infinite tail $c$ to each vertex of that path gives a finite chain of pairs of the preceding
special form, with either orientation. Symmetry and transitivity of $E$ give $\pi(a)\mathrel E\pi(b)$.
This proves both directions of the reduction on Baire space. For a general Polish $X$, take a closed
$F\IncludedIn\BaireSpace$ and a continuous Borel isomorphism from $F$ onto $X$, as constructed in
§{sec:closed-presentation-baire}. Pull $E$ back to $F$ and extend it to Baire space by putting all points outside
$F$ into one new class. This extension is Borel and nonsmooth: a complete Borel invariant for it would restrict
to one for $E$. Apply the Baire-space result. The inverse image of $F$ under the resulting embedding is closed
and $\EventualEqualityER$-invariant. It is nonempty, since otherwise all images would be equivalent. Every nonempty
closed $\EventualEqualityER$-invariant subset of Cantor space is the whole space: changing increasingly long
prefixes of one member approximates any prescribed binary sequence. Hence the embedding takes values in $F$.
Composing with the continuous Borel isomorphism onto $X$ preserves continuity, injectivity, and the reduction
equivalence.
\end{proof}

The deduction of the \termuse{Glimm--Effros dichotomy} in §{sec:e0-glimm-effros} now uses this proved embedding.
The node conditions retain point witnesses, the edge conditions retain equivalence witnesses, and the restricted
rectangles separate branches with infinitely many disagreements. These are three distinct roles; ordinary diameter
shrinking alone supplies none of the two relation conclusions.

\part{Computation and Realizability}\label{part-computation-realizability}

This part returns to the names and representations developed in §{sec:cauchy-representation-admissibility}
and asks which constructions can be carried out from those names. Type-Two Effectivity supplies lambda
realizers for maps between represented spaces. The following chapters apply this language to computable
analysis and algorithmic randomness, and use Weihrauch reducibility to compare the information needed to
solve multivalued problems.

Domain theory then studies partial information and connects it to admissible representations. The final two
chapters develop realizability models and the effective topos, where realizers give computational meaning to
statements and maps. The tripos-to-topos construction also connects this interpretation with forcing-valued
predicates. Readers can start this part directly from the representation tools of Part I; the chapter entry
guides identify the computational and categorical background needed along the way.
